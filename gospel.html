The Question Every Christian Must Confront

Every day, millions of Christians gather in churches, attend religious meetings, watch sermons, listen to preachers, and study the Holy Scriptures. Across the world, countless messages are delivered in the name of God, each claiming to communicate divine truth.

Yet, beneath this widespread religious activity lies a question of profound spiritual importance:

Who is the source of the gospel you hear?

Is the message you receive truly grounded in the revelation of Jesus Christ, or has it been shaped by human imagination, personal reasoning, religious traditions, and interpretations that originate from man?

This question is not intended to discourage believers from listening to preachers or receiving instruction. Rather, it invites every Christian to examine the foundation of what they believe, why they believe it, and whether those beliefs are consistent with the gospel revealed in Scripture.

A message may contain biblical quotations and still communicate ideas that do not faithfully represent the meaning of those Scriptures.

Therefore, the presence of Bible verses in a sermon is not, by itself, sufficient evidence that the sermon communicates the mind of God.

The important question is not merely whether Scripture has been quoted, but whether Scripture has been faithfully understood and communicated.

The Apostle Paul's Declaration: Not After Man

In his letter to the Galatians, the Apostle Paul makes a remarkable declaration concerning the origin of the gospel he preached:

"But I certify you, brethren, that the gospel which was preached of me is not after man. For I neither received it of man, neither was I taught it, but by the revelation of Jesus Christ."

Galatians 1:11-12 (KJV)

These words form the foundation of our examination.

Paul identifies three important characteristics of the gospel he received.

First, the gospel was not after man.

Second, he did not receive it from man.

Third, it came by the revelation of Jesus Christ.

Paul was defending the divine origin of his apostolic message. His declaration points to a distinction between a gospel originating in God's revelation and teachings invented or distorted by human beings.

This distinction deserves serious attention because the gospel is not merely a collection of religious opinions. It concerns the person, death, resurrection, and saving work of Jesus Christ.

In Paul's own experience, his understanding of Christ was radically transformed through an encounter with the risen Lord. The one who had previously persecuted the Church became a witness to the very gospel he had opposed.

His authority did not arise from inventing a new religious philosophy. He proclaimed what had been revealed to him concerning Jesus Christ.

Nevertheless, Paul's unique apostolic experience does not mean that every authentic preacher must receive the gospel through an independent supernatural encounter. The New Testament also establishes the importance of faithful teachers, discipleship, and the transmission of apostolic instruction.

What matters is that the message ultimately remains faithful to Christ and His gospel.

The Danger of a Gospel Shaped by Human Understanding

Human understanding is valuable, but it is limited. Human beings interpret the world through education, culture, experience, traditions, personal convictions, and inherited beliefs.

These influences can assist our understanding, but they can also distort it when they become more authoritative than the Scriptures themselves.

One of the greatest dangers in biblical interpretation arises when a person approaches Scripture with an already established conclusion and then searches for passages that appear to support it.

Instead of allowing Scripture to shape their beliefs, they reshape the meaning of Scripture to accommodate their beliefs.

This is how personal opinions can gradually become religious doctrines.

An interpretation may sound convincing, be delivered eloquently, and receive widespread acceptance while still failing to reflect the intended meaning of the biblical passage.

As the original sermon warns, there is a possibility of introducing our own ideas into Scripture and presenting them to others as the mind of God.

This possibility should concern both those who preach and those who listen.

Human confidence does not automatically establish divine truth. Neither does religious popularity guarantee biblical accuracy.

The Apostle Paul expressed grave concern when believers were being drawn toward teachings that distorted the gospel of Christ. In Galatians 1:6-9, he warns against accepting a different gospel.

This warning demonstrates that not every message presented under the name of Christianity should automatically be accepted as faithful Christian doctrine.

A believer must learn to distinguish between what Scripture actually teaches and what an individual claims that Scripture teaches.

Divine Revelation and the Understanding of Scripture

If human understanding can be limited, how then should believers approach the Scriptures?

The Bible consistently emphasizes the importance of God's Spirit in bringing understanding to His people.

In 1 Corinthians 2:10-14, Paul explains the relationship between divine revelation, the Holy Spirit, and spiritual discernment.

This passage teaches that the knowledge of God's purposes is not simply the product of unaided human wisdom.

The Holy Spirit enables believers to recognize and understand spiritual truth.

However, the work of the Spirit should not be confused with the freedom to assign any meaning we desire to a biblical text.

True spiritual understanding must not contradict the truth that the Spirit has already revealed through Scripture.

A claim of revelation must therefore be approached with discernment rather than accepted solely because an individual says that God revealed something to them.

The Apostle John gives an important instruction:

"Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God"

1 John 4:1 (KJV)

The instruction is clear: believers are not required to accept every spiritual claim without examination.

Indeed, discernment is an important responsibility of Christian faith.

Divine revelation is not an excuse to abandon careful reading, sound interpretation, or responsible biblical study. These practices are essential when seeking to understand and communicate the Scriptures faithfully.

The objective is not to choose between the Spirit of God and the written Word of God, but to recognize their proper relationship in Christian understanding.

Why Are There So Many Different Interpretations?

Today, Christians encounter numerous interpretations of the same biblical passages.

Different preachers may read the same verse and arrive at significantly different conclusions. In some cases, these differences concern secondary matters. In others, they involve fundamental questions about the gospel itself.

Why does this happen?

The original sermon raises an important possibility: some interpretations may arise predominantly from human assumptions rather than a faithful understanding of divine revelation.

This is a concern worth examining.

However, differences in interpretation can also arise from language, historical context, theological traditions, imperfect knowledge, and the complexity of certain biblical passages. Disagreement alone does not prove that one person possesses divine revelation while another is deliberately teaching human invention.

The more important question is whether an interpretation is supported by the full witness of Scripture.

Does it respect the context of the passage? Does it agree with the wider biblical teaching concerning Jesus Christ? Does it preserve the meaning intended by the biblical author?

Or does it depend on isolated words, selective quotations, and assumptions introduced into the text?

A faithful interpretation should seek to understand Scripture rather than force Scripture to confirm a preferred conclusion.

The goal of studying the Bible is not to prove ourselves right, but to understand what God has revealed.

The Responsibility of the Listener

One of the most challenging questions in the original sermon concerns the responsibility of ordinary Christians.

Many believers regularly listen to preaching. Yet how frequently do they examine what has been preached?

How often do they open their Bibles after a sermon to investigate whether the message accurately represents the passage being discussed?

The Book of Acts provides an instructive example.

Concerning the believers in Berea, Scripture states that they:

"searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so."

Acts 17:11 (KJV)

These believers did not reject preaching, nor did they receive it without examination.

They listened with openness and investigated the Scriptures.

Remarkably, the teaching they examined came from the Apostle Paul himself.

If the Bereans examined the preaching of Paul, how much more should believers today carefully consider the teachings they receive?

Respect for a preacher and examination of a preacher's message are not mutually exclusive.

A faithful teacher should welcome sincere biblical inquiry rather than demand unquestioning acceptance.

Paul also instructed Timothy to be diligent in handling the Word of truth correctly, emphasizing the responsibility involved in teaching Scripture.

The listener, likewise, must not surrender personal discernment simply because someone speaks with confidence, occupies a religious office, possesses extensive knowledge, or commands a large following.

The authority of a Christian message must be established by its faithfulness to Christ and Scripture, not merely by the reputation of the person delivering it.

What We Believe Determines the Direction We Follow

There is another profound observation in the original message: what a person hears and comes to believe can influence the direction of their life.

Beliefs shape decisions.

Decisions shape actions.

Actions, repeated over time, shape character and influence the future.

Therefore, the teachings we continuously receive can have consequences far beyond a religious gathering.

A distorted understanding of Scripture can produce distorted expectations, practices, and attitudes. Conversely, a sound understanding of biblical truth can encourage humility, repentance, love, obedience, and spiritual maturity.

This is why believers must be intentional about the messages they allow to shape their understanding of God.

Christian faith should not be built upon the personality of a preacher or the emotional appeal of a sermon alone.

Its foundation must remain Jesus Christ, whose gospel the apostles proclaimed.

The purpose of examining teachings is not to cultivate suspicion toward every preacher. It is to develop spiritual maturity, sound judgment, and a deeper commitment to truth.

The Final Question: What Is the Source of Your Gospel?

The Apostle Paul's words in Galatians 1:11-12 remain deeply relevant.

His testimony challenges believers to recognize that the gospel of Jesus Christ is not a human invention and must not be reduced to one.

Yet this also places a responsibility upon everyone who teaches, interprets, or receives the Scriptures.

The preacher must be careful not to present personal opinions as unquestionable divine revelation.

The listener must be careful not to accept every interpretation simply because it is accompanied by biblical quotations.

Both must approach Scripture with humility, seriousness, prayer, and a sincere desire for truth.

The question is therefore not only whether a preacher can quote the Bible.

It is whether the message accurately communicates the gospel of Jesus Christ.

Before accepting a doctrine, examine its biblical foundation. Before repeating an interpretation, investigate its context. Before defending a teaching, consider whether it truly expresses the truth revealed in Scripture.

And above all, remember that the goal of Christian understanding is not merely the accumulation of religious knowledge.

It is to know Jesus Christ, remain faithful to His gospel, and allow His truth to transform our lives.

So the question remains:

Who is the source of the gospel you hear?

Is it founded upon the revelation of Jesus Christ, or has it been shaped into something else by the ideas of man?

Every believer has a responsibility to examine that question.

And every preacher has a responsibility to ensure that the message they proclaim remains faithful to the One whose name they preach.

Questions for Personal Reflection and Bible Study

1. What did Paul mean when he declared that the gospel he preached was not after man?

2. What is the difference between the divine origin of the gospel and the human responsibility to teach it faithfully?

3. Can a preacher quote Scripture accurately but interpret it incorrectly?

4. How can believers distinguish biblical teaching from personal opinion presented as revelation?

5. What can Christians learn from the example of the Bereans?

6. What role should the Holy Spirit play in understanding Scripture?

7. How can believers test spiritual claims without rejecting genuine spiritual instruction?

8. What is the relationship between sound doctrine and spiritual transformation?

9. How should a Christian respond when two respected preachers offer contradictory interpretations?

10. Most importantly, is the gospel shaping your beliefs truly the gospel of Jesus Christ?
